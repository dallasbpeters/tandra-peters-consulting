## Design Context

### Users

**Primary:** Texas homeowners across Austin, Central Texas, and West Texas (Amarillo, Lubbock and the surrounding metros). Two states of mind dominate:

1. **Reactive — post-storm.** A hailstorm or wind event just hit. They're on their phone in the driveway, photographing a downed gutter, googling "Austin roofer" while the insurance call is still fresh. They are mildly panicked and acutely allergic to being sold to.
2. **Proactive — planning ahead.** A 12–20 year-old roof, a real estate transaction looming, or a seasonal "is my roof okay?" check. Slower research, often on desktop, comparing 3+ consultants.

Both states need the same thing first: **proof that the person on the other end is trustworthy, knowledgeable, and won't pressure them.** The competitive set is loud and salesy; calm and substantive is the differentiator.

### Brand Personality

Tandra is the **friendly Texas straight-talker who happens to know roofing inside and out** — the neighbor at the barbecue with the unusual combination of mom energy and pitch-deck-grade technical depth. Visually that means:

- **Editorial structure as the container** — typographic discipline, generous whitespace, restrained color, no shouting. The site itself reads as considered.
- **Neighborly warmth in the micro-details** — photo selection (real Texas roofs, real Tandra, not stock houses-with-gradients), conversational microcopy, real numbers in pull-quotes, the founder's portrait as a circle in the nav (the one rounded element in an otherwise sharp-edged system).

Three-word personality: **steady, plainspoken, considered.**

Emotional goal at every touchpoint: the visitor exhales. They were braced for a hard sell and got a competent neighbor instead.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Visual reference (positive):** [AngelList / Wellfound](https://angellist.com) — structural restraint, strong typographic hierarchy, density without clutter, near-monochrome surfaces with one disciplined accent. Take its **container discipline** and add the **warmth** that AngelList intentionally lacks (photo, real human portraiture, the warm-cream paper surface, the hand-set Instrument Serif moments).

**Theme:** Light. Warm limestone paper (`#F4F4F1`) is the default surface because the audience reads in daylight on phones in driveways, on tablets at kitchen tables, on laptops at coffee shops. Dark surfaces (`#141410`) appear intentionally for hero bands and article cards — moments where photography should carry the eye.

**Anti-references (the local-roofer failure modes we won't touch):**

- Generic stock photography of houses with blue-sky + dramatic gradient overlays
- Giant yellow / red "CALL NOW" banners, storm-chaser urgency, "$500 OFF!!!" exit-intent modals
- Wall-of-trust-badges hero crowding (BBB, Angie's, manufacturer logos stacked four-wide)
- Stale corporate-blue + clip-art shingle iconography
- **Cluttered before/after carousels with bad cropping.** If we show before/ after, it's a single curated pair with intent, not a slider.
- Anything that would look at home on the first page of Google results for "Austin roofer."

**Sharp edges, deliberately.** Buttons, badges, nav chrome, form inputs — no border radius. The only rounded elements are article cards (`16px`) and the founder avatar (full circle). The flat-edge default is the visual signature.

### Design Principles

1. **Quiet authority over loud persuasion.** If a competitor would yell it, we either don't say it or say it in 11px label-caps at the bottom of a section. Every flourish must be quieter than the trade norm.
2. **Editorial container, neighborly contents.** The page structure reads as _New Yorker_; the words, photos, and micro-interactions read as Tandra at the barbecue. Don't soften the chrome; warm the contents.
3. **One bright thing per page.** The hero CTA, or the article hero photo, or the periwinkle headline accent — pick one and let it be the brightest object. Every page has a clear visual answer to "where do I look first?"
4. **Real numbers over reassuring adjectives.** "15–20 years," "110+ mph rated," "1.7 hours average response time" — the typography is built to showcase numerals (set them in IBM Plex Serif at scale for emphasis).
5. **Restraint is the budget.** When in doubt, remove. No card-within-card, no extra section, no decorative SVG that doesn't earn its weight. Density without clutter is the AngelList lesson.

### Accessibility Bar

- **WCAG 2.1 AA** across all surfaces (already enforced in `DESIGN.md` via documented contrast pairings).
- **`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`** must be honored — no fade-ups, staggers, parallax, or scroll-driven reveals when set.
- **Keyboard-navigable end-to-end** with visible focus rings (`outline: 2px solid var(--everglade); outline-offset: 3px`, already in `Nav.tsx`).
- **Semantic HTML first.** No `<div onClick>` for things that should be buttons or links. Form fields paired with `<label>`. Headings descend without skipping levels.
